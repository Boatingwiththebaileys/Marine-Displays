Marine Displays help

1. What you need

  • One of the supported Waveshare ESP32-S3 displays (see the installer for the exact boards).
  • A USB-C data cable. Some cables only carry power; if your computer can't see the display, try another cable.
  • A computer with Chrome or Edge for installing. Safari and Firefox can't install firmware.
  • A data source: a Signal K server on the boat's WiFi (for example on a Raspberry Pi or Victron Cerbo), or for the NMEA 2000 display, the boat's NMEA 2000 network.
  • Optional but recommended: a microSD card, 8–32 GB, formatted FAT32, for your own icons and background images. See SD card.
↑ Top

2. Install the firmware

  1. Open the installer in Chrome or Edge.
  2. Plug the display into the computer.
  3. Click Connect on the card for your display, choose the port that appears, then Install. It takes about a minute.

On a brand-new display, tick Erase device. When updating a display you've already set up, leave it unticked so your settings are kept.

TipIf no port appears, hold the BOOT button on the board while plugging the cable in, then click Connect again.

3. First-time setup

Until it knows your boat's WiFi, the display starts its own WiFi network so you can set it up:

DisplaySetup WiFi networkPassword
4" Square (Signal K and NMEA 2000)ESP32-SquareDisplay12345678
2.8" and 2.1" RoundESP32-RoundDisplay12345678
4.2" RLCDESP32-RLCDDisplay12345678
  1. On your phone or laptop, join that network.
  2. Open a browser and go to http://192.168.4.1.
  3. Open Network Setup. Use Scan to pick your boat's WiFi, and enter its password.
  4. For Signal K displays, enter the Signal K server IP address and port. The port is usually 3000; check your server's settings.
  5. Give the display a hostname you'll recognise, such as helm-square.
  6. Click Save & Reboot. The display restarts and joins your WiFi.
TipGive the Signal K server a fixed IP address in your router (often called a "DHCP reservation") so the display can always find it.

4. Finding your display on the boat's WiFi

After setup, the display's web page is at its own IP address on your boat's network. To find it:

  • Touchscreen displays: swipe down on the screen to open the Settings panel, which shows the IP address and WiFi signal.
  • Try http://hostname.local, using the hostname you set (works on most phones and computers).
  • Look for the hostname in your router's list of connected devices.
TipBookmark each display's page on your phone. With several displays, a hostname that says where it's fitted (nav-station, helm) saves guesswork.

5. Using the display

Each display has 5 screens you can set up however you like.

Touchscreen displays (Square and Round)

  • Swipe left / right to move between screens.
  • Swipe down to open the Settings panel: IP address, WiFi signal, buzzer, auto-scroll and (on the Square) screen sleep and brightness.
  • Swipe up to close the Settings panel.

4.2" RLCD

The RLCD has no touchscreen. Choose which screen it shows from the Screens buttons on its web page, or turn on Auto-scroll to cycle through them.

TipAuto-scroll (5–60 seconds) turns a single display into a rotating dashboard, which is handy where nobody can reach the screen.

9. Gauges, zones and alarms

Calibration points

Each gauge has 5 calibration points, each pairing a value with a needle angle. The needle moves smoothly between them, so you can match backgrounds that aren't evenly spaced.

Gauge values use raw data unitsCalibration points and alarm zones work on the raw value from the data source, not the units chosen in Device Settings. On Signal K displays these are Signal K's base units:
  • RPM is revolutions per second: 3000 RPM = 50
  • Temperatures are Kelvin: 90 °C = 363.15
  • Pressures are Pascals: 4 bar = 400000
  • Tank levels are 0 to 1: half full = 0.5
  • Speeds are metres per second: 10 knots ≈ 5.14

On the NMEA 2000 display, RPM is plain RPM, but temperatures and pressures are still Kelvin and Pascals. Number, Dual and Quad displays convert values for you automatically.

Zones and alerts

Each gauge has up to 4 zones, each a Min–Max range with a Color. When the value is in a zone, the gauge's icon turns that colour (tick Transparent to show the icon in its own colours instead). Tick Buzzer on a zone to sound the buzzer while the value is in it. A typical coolant gauge has a normal zone, a warm zone in amber, and an overheat zone in red with the buzzer on.

Tip: overlapping zonesIf zones overlap, the narrowest one wins. So you can set a wide "normal" zone across the whole scale, then a narrow red zone at the top for the alarm, without worrying about exact boundaries.

Number displays have simpler Low and High alarm values.

Buzzer

  • Off: no sound.
  • Global: alarms on any screen sound the buzzer.
  • Per-screen: only the screen being shown can sound it.

Buzzer cooldown sets how long it waits between beeps: constant, or a 5–60 second pause.

TipUse white or light-grey PNG icons. The zone colours are applied to the icon, so a white icon shows green, amber and red clearly.

10. SD card, icons and backgrounds

The card

  • Use a microSD card of 8–32 GB. These come formatted FAT32, which is what the display needs.
  • exFAT doesn't work. Cards of 64 GB and above usually come as exFAT and need reformatting to FAT32. On a Mac, use Disk Utility → Erase → MS-DOS (FAT). Windows' own formatter can't do FAT32 above 32 GB, so you'll need a FAT32 formatting tool. A card in the wrong format is simply ignored; it is never erased.
  • Large cards also make the Round displays slower to start, because they check the card's free space at boot.

Folders

  • Put icons and backgrounds in a folder called assets at the top of the card.
  • The Square displays also keep a backup of your screen settings in a config folder. See Backups.

Icons

  • PNG files, 70 × 70 pixels, ideally white on a transparent background so zone colours show well.
  • Upload them on the Assets page, or copy them into assets with a card reader.

Backgrounds

  • The same size as the screen: 480 × 480 on the Square and Round displays.
  • Saved as a raw RGB565 .bin file. Design the background as a PNG, then convert it with the convert_png_to_rgb565.py script in the display's folder on GitHub:
    python3 convert_png_to_rgb565.py my_background.png my_background.bin
  • Copy the .bin into assets with a card reader.
Tip: start from the examplesThe assets folder on GitHub has ready-made backgrounds (rev counter, temperatures, depth, wind, batteries and more) and icons. Copy them onto your card to get going, then design your own.
Tip: line up gaugesWhen designing a gauge background, note the angle of each scale mark. Those angles go straight into the calibration points.

12. NMEA 2000 display

The NMEA 2000 version of the 4" Square reads data straight from the boat's NMEA 2000 network, so no Signal K server is needed. Instead of typing Signal K paths, choose each value from a list of NMEA 2000 fields (engine, depth, wind, GPS, AIS, batteries and more).

  • Network Setup shows a live count of NMEA 2000 messages received, which is a quick check that the bus is connected.
  • The display only listens; it never sends anything onto the NMEA 2000 bus.
  • The beta version can also take its data from a WiFi gateway (Yacht Devices, Actisense or SeaSmart) instead of a cable. See the Beta section of the installer and Network Setup → NMEA 2000 data source.

13. Updating and backups

Updating

Use the installer with Erase device unticked. Your WiFi, screens and settings are kept.

Don't use installer files on the Firmware Update pageThe .bin files from the installer and the GitHub releases are complete flash images for USB installing. Don't upload them on the display's own Firmware Update page.

Backups

  • On the Square displays, screen settings are also saved to the SD card (config folder) and loaded from there first. Erasing the display won't lose your screens while that card is in.
  • To start the screens completely fresh, delete the config folder from the card as well as installing with Erase device ticked.
  • Keep a copy of your assets and config folders on your computer.

14. Troubleshooting

ProblemTry this
Installer shows no portUse a different USB-C cable (some are power-only). Hold BOOT while plugging in. On Windows you may need the USB-serial driver for the board.
Screen blank after installingUnplug and plug back in.
Can't see the setup WiFi networkIt only appears when the display can't join a saved WiFi network, so give it a minute after power-on. If it had joined another network, use that network to reach its page.
No data on screenCheck the Signal K server IP and port on Network Setup, and that the display and server are on the same WiFi. Copy paths from the server's Data Browser, since a single wrong letter means no data.
Needle stuck at the end of the gaugeThe calibration values are probably in the wrong units. See Gauge values use raw data units.
Icons or backgrounds missing from the listsCheck the card is FAT32 (not exFAT), the folder is named assets at the top of the card, icons are .png and backgrounds are .bin.
Forgot the display's addressSwipe down on the screen to see the IP address, or try http://hostname.local.
NMEA 2000 display shows "No data"Check the NMEA 2000 connection and that the network is powered.

Still stuck? Open an issue on GitHub, saying which display you have and what you've tried.
